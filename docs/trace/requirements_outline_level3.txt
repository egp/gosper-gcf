1. Internal Exact GCF Representation
1.1 All internal computation shall use generalized (p,q) continued-fraction streams.
1.1.1 Internal source interfaces shall expose values as generalized PQ streams.
1.1.2 Internal transform kernels shall consume and produce exact state over generalized PQ semantics.
1.1.3 Internal composition shall preserve generalized-stream semantics until final client emission.

1.2 Regular continued fractions shall be treated as the q=1 special case.
1.2.1 Any RCF-compatible source shall be representable as a PQ stream with q=1.
1.2.2 RCF adapters shall not introduce alternate arithmetic semantics distinct from PQ semantics.

1.3 Only the final client-facing boundary shall emit regular continued-fraction terms.
1.3.1 Internal components shall not expose speculative client-visible RCF digits directly.
1.3.2 Any internal RCF-like prefixes used for feedback or adaptation shall remain non-client-facing until certified.

1.4 Internal sources, adapters, generators, kernels, and transducers shall all conform to the generalized GCF model.
1.4.1 Built-in named sources shall enter the system through generalized-stream-compatible adapters.
1.4.2 Unary, binary, and diagonal kernels shall interoperate through exact stream/state contracts.

2. Certified Client-Facing Output
2.1 Client-visible output shall consist only of certified regular continued-fraction terms.
2.1.1 Each emitted client term shall be an exact integer RCF term.
2.1.2 Client-visible emission shall occur only at the certified-output boundary.

2.2 No speculative client-visible terms shall be emitted.
2.2.1 A term shall not be exposed to the client merely because it appears likely.
2.2.2 Internal narrowing without proof shall not justify client-visible output.

2.3 No retractable client-visible terms shall be emitted.
2.3.1 Once emitted, a client-visible term shall remain valid under all later refinement.
2.3.2 Later source ingestion shall not invalidate earlier client-visible terms.

2.4 A client-visible term shall be emitted only when its floor is proven stable over the full admissible image.
2.4.1 Certification shall consider the full current admissible image, not a sample point.
2.4.2 Equality of floor across the admissible image shall be sufficient for emission.
2.4.3 Failure to prove floor stability shall prevent emission.

3. Core Transform Kernels and Output Rectification
3.1 The system shall implement a unified bihomographic kernel (BLFT) with exact bigint coefficients.
3.1.1 BLFT coefficients shall be maintained using exact integer arithmetic only.
3.1.2 BLFT state transitions shall preserve exactness under ingest and emission steps.

3.2 The BLFT shall support binary arithmetic and unary specializations.
3.2.1 Binary operators shall be representable through BLFT state over two inputs.
3.2.2 Unary operators that are linear-fractional shall be representable as BLFT specializations.

3.3 The system shall implement a diagonal quadratic kernel (DLFT) with exact bigint coefficients.
3.3.1 DLFT coefficients shall be maintained using exact integer arithmetic only.
3.3.2 DLFT state transitions shall preserve exactness under Y-term ingestion and coefficient update.

3.4 The DLFT shall be available for square and sqrt-related workflows.
3.4.1 The Newton refinement map for sqrt shall be encoded as a DLFT.
3.4.2 The square-related unary path may use DLFT-compatible machinery where required.

3.5 The system shall provide an output rectifier at the client-facing boundary.
3.5.1 The rectifier shall observe the current admissible image before emission.
3.5.2 The rectifier shall block output when certification conditions are not met.

3.6 The rectifier shall absorb speculative internal terms and emit only certified client-visible RCF terms.
3.6.1 Internal progress may continue without client-visible emission.
3.6.2 Rectification shall preserve exactness and non-retractability guarantees.

3.7 Kernel coefficient tuples shall be normalized by exact GCD reduction when representation-preserving.
3.7.1 Normalization shall not change the mathematical transform represented by the kernel.
3.7.2 Normalization shall not introduce division by non-common factors.

4. Demand-Driven Lazy Evaluation
4.1 The system shall request source terms only when needed to force certified progress.
4.1.1 No source shall be preread solely to simplify implementation.
4.1.2 Source consumption shall be justified by a current certification or refinement need.

4.2 The system shall emit output terms as soon as they are provably certified.
4.2.1 A certified term shall not be withheld once the next-output condition is met.
4.2.2 Emission readiness shall be reevaluated after each state-changing step.

4.3 Public evaluation of composed expressions shall remain lazy.
4.3.1 Constructing an operator pipeline shall not require exhausting its inputs.
4.3.2 Lazy behavior shall hold for finite, rational, and streaming irrational inputs.

4.4 Input-ingest decisions shall follow Gosper’s widest-range rule, or an exact equivalent.
4.4.1 When multiple ingests are possible, the chosen ingest shall be justified by exact range information.
4.4.2 Any equivalent ingest policy shall preserve correctness and certified-output semantics.

4.5 The ingest-ordering policy shall be deterministic under equal observable state.
4.5.1 Repeated runs from the same state shall make the same ingest choice.
4.5.2 Determinism shall hold absent external randomness or non-deterministic resource failure.

5. Exact Interval / Range Semantics
5.1 The system shall expose the current exact interval alongside next-term emission.
5.1.1 Public observation of current range shall not require future source consumption.
5.1.2 Reported current range shall reflect the exact current admissible set.

5.2 Range representation shall support exact rational endpoints.
5.2.1 Endpoint values shall be represented without floating-point approximation.
5.2.2 Endpoint comparison shall use exact rational ordering.

5.3 Range representation shall support endpoint openness flags.
5.3.1 Each endpoint shall carry open/closed status explicitly.
5.3.2 Openness shall participate in normalization and equality rules.

5.4 Range representation shall support inside/outside polarity on the projective line.
5.4.1 Outside ranges shall be first-class values, not approximated as large finite intervals.
5.4.2 Projective-line semantics shall remain consistent through transform propagation.

5.5 BLFT range propagation shall use exact corner-derived image analysis and exact union.
5.5.1 Corner images shall be computed exactly from admissible endpoint data.
5.5.2 When the image is disjoint, exact union semantics shall be preserved.

5.6 Denominator-crossing behavior shall be handled exactly.
5.6.1 Denominator zeros within the admissible domain shall be detected exactly.
5.6.2 Crossing-induced outside-range results shall be represented explicitly.

5.7 Outside-range semantics shall be preserved as first-class behavior, not approximated away.
5.7.1 Normalization shall not collapse outside-ranges into lossy finite surrogates.
5.7.2 Certification logic shall account for outside-range structure when computing floors.

6. Source Exhaustion, Tail Semantics, and Exact Rational Termination
6.1 Finite-source exhaustion shall be handled by Gosper’s tail-limit rule with x→∞ or y→∞ collapse.
6.1.1 EOF collapse shall use the appropriate projective tail interpretation for the exhausted side.
6.1.2 EOF collapse shall preserve exactness of the remaining live transform.

6.2 Exhaustion shall preserve the live kernel representation rather than switching to an unrelated path.
6.2.1 Unary/binary collapse shall remain expressed through the existing kernel state when feasible.
6.2.2 Exhaustion handling shall not introduce ad hoc non-kernel semantics.

6.3 Exact rational isolation shall trigger Rational Collapse.
6.3.1 When the admissible set is a single exact rational, rational-collapse entry conditions shall hold.
6.3.2 Rational collapse shall not begin before exact singleton isolation is proven.

6.4 Rational Collapse shall emit the finite exact RCF sequence and terminate.
6.4.1 The emitted finite sequence shall equal the exact RCF of the isolated rational.
6.4.2 After the final term, the stream shall terminate cleanly.

6.5 The engine shall either make certified progress or fail explicitly when configured resource limits are exceeded.
6.5.1 Lack of immediate progress alone shall not justify silent looping.
6.5.2 Resource-limit failure shall be explicit and observable to the caller.

7. Built-In Sources, Adapters, and Non-sqrt Unary/Binary Operations
7.1 The system shall provide built-in GCF generators for pi and e.
7.1.1 Pi generation shall use an exact continued-fraction-compatible construction.
7.1.2 E generation shall use an exact continued-fraction-compatible construction.

7.2 The system shall provide adapters between required stream forms, including RCF-to-PQ and exact rational sources.
7.2.1 PQStreamFromRCF shall convert an exact RCF prefix/stream into PQ terms.
7.2.2 Exact rational values shall be injectable through dedicated rational-source adapters.

7.3 The system shall provide unary operators needed for the MVP expression, including square and reciprocal.
7.3.1 Reciprocal shall preserve exactness and certified emission semantics.
7.3.2 Square shall preserve exactness and integrate with interval-based certification.

7.4 The system shall provide trig and hyperbolic operators needed for the MVP expression.
7.4.1 Sine-in-radians support shall remain exact and lazily composable.
7.4.2 Tanh support shall remain exact and lazily composable.

7.5 The core package shall remain generic and free of named- or trig-specific policy.
7.5.1 Core shall not encode source-specific hacks for named constants.
7.5.2 Core shall not encode trig-specific heuristics or identities.

7.6 SinDegrees shall be implemented as an exact degree-to-radian wrapper around the radians-based sine operator.
7.6.1 Degree inputs shall be converted exactly to radians before sine evaluation.
7.6.2 The degrees wrapper shall not alter the underlying certified-output contract.

7.7 Any exact rational shortcut behavior shall remain explicit and bounded by documented requirements.
7.7.1 Shortcut paths shall not violate laziness where laziness is required by contract.
7.7.2 Shortcut paths shall not bypass certification guarantees.

8. HAKMEM 101C Smallest-Rational-in-Interval Utility
8.1 The system shall implement the HAKMEM 101C smallest-rational-in-interval procedure.
8.1.1 The procedure shall operate using exact arithmetic only.
8.1.2 The procedure shall return a rational witness admissible under the exact interval contract.

8.2 The 101C utility shall operate on any nonempty exact interval admissible under the projective range model.
8.2.1 Open and closed endpoints shall be respected exactly.
8.2.2 Outside-range/projective cases shall be handled when they are valid under the interval model.

8.3 The 101C utility shall be usable for Rational Collapse.
8.3.1 Rational-collapse logic may use the 101C utility to identify canonical witnesses.
8.3.2 Use of the 101C utility shall preserve exact termination behavior.

8.4 The 101C utility shall be usable for canonical witness generation in tests and diagnostics.
8.4.1 White-box and black-box tests may use 101C witnesses for exact expectations.
8.4.2 Diagnostic output may report 101C-selected witnesses when helpful.

9. Resource Limits and Explicit Failure Modes
9.1 The system shall provide caller-configurable time/work/resource guards.
9.1.1 The caller shall be able to bound evaluation effort.
9.1.2 Guard configuration shall not weaken correctness guarantees.

9.2 The system shall support limits related to coefficient or bit-length growth.
9.2.1 Kernel/state growth may be bounded explicitly by configuration.
9.2.2 Growth-limit handling shall fail explicitly rather than silently truncating exact state.

9.3 The system may support limits on emitted term count as an additional guard.
9.3.1 Term-count limits, if provided, shall be enforced without violating emitted-term correctness.
9.3.2 Term-count exhaustion shall surface as an explicit stop/error condition.

9.4 Resource exhaustion shall return an explicit error rather than silently looping.
9.4.1 Exhaustion errors shall be distinguishable from ordinary EOF termination.
9.4.2 Exhaustion errors shall not expose uncertified partial output as certified.

9.5 Public failure modes shall preserve exactness and non-retractable-output guarantees.
9.5.1 Failure shall not retroactively invalidate previously emitted certified terms.
9.5.2 Failure handling shall not substitute approximate results for exact ones.

10. Sqrt as an Ouroboros Feedback Controller
10.1 Sqrt(X) shall be implemented as an exact, lazy, Gosper-style square-root operator.
10.1.1 Sqrt shall use exact arithmetic only and shall not use floating-point seeding or refinement.
10.1.2 Sqrt shall consume radicand terms on demand rather than prereading the input.
10.1.3 Client-visible sqrt output shall consist only of certified RCF terms.
10.1.4 The inner feedback loop shall be named ourorobos and shall be WB-visible.

10.2 Sqrt(X) shall be implemented as a feedback stream controller, not as a BLFT.
10.2.1 The emerging output prefix shall serve as the live approximation stream fed back into refinement.
10.2.2 The sqrt controller shall not duplicate-consume a live stream to simulate BLFT composition.

10.3 Sqrt(X) shall not be modeled as an ordinary DLFT input-stream problem with X as a second live DLFT input.
10.3.1 The Y approximation stream shall be ingested into the DLFT as the live variable stream.
10.3.2 The radicand X shall contribute through coefficient selection/update rather than as a second live DLFT stream.

10.4 Newton refinement shall be the governing refinement map for the sqrt controller.
10.4.1 The controller shall implement Y_{n+1} = (Y_n + X/Y_n)/2.
10.4.2 For rational X = n/d, the controller shall encode refinement as the DLFT (dY^2 + n)/(2dY).
10.4.3 The DLFT coefficients for rational X shall be initialized as (A=d, B=0, C=n, D=0, E=2d, F=0).

10.5 The sqrt design shall use the DLFT kernel for Newton refinement.
10.5.1 BLFT-only composition shall not be accepted as an equivalent implementation of the Newton kernel.
10.5.2 The 1/2 factor of Newton refinement shall be represented in DLFT state rather than by a separate required half stream.

10.6 The sqrt design shall avoid preread-to-EOF behavior.
10.6.1 Sqrt shall not determine finiteness by exhausting X up front.
10.6.2 EOF, if reached, shall be handled through ordinary core collapse semantics.

10.7 The sqrt design shall avoid finite-only shortcut paths and source-specific special knowledge.
10.7.1 Sqrt shall not special-case named sources as a substitute for the general feedback design.
10.7.2 Any exact rational shortcut retained shall remain explicit and bounded by documented requirements.

10.8 The sqrt controller shall rely on ordinary EOF collapse for finite radicands.
10.8.1 If X exhausts before sqrt resolution completes, standard core tail-to-infinity collapse shall apply.
10.8.2 EOF handling shall not discard accrued sqrt-controller progress.

11. Sqrt Input Contract, Operational Rules, and Signoff
11.1 Sqrt shall validate its input contract before or at first observable evaluation step.
11.1.1 A nil input stream shall produce an error.
11.1.2 A current range that proves X < 0 shall produce an error.
11.1.3 A current range that allows non-negative values shall permit evaluation to proceed.
11.1.4 An exact X = 0 input shall emit 0 and terminate.
11.1.5 Any PQ term with P < 0 or Q < 0 shall produce an error.
11.1.6 The exact error surface shall be consistent with the rest of the core API.

11.2 Sqrt seed selection shall use only currently available exact X information.
11.2.1 The initial seed shall be a strictly positive exact rational.
11.2.2 Seed selection shall not require lookahead beyond currently available X information.
11.2.3 Allowed seed strategies include a value derived from the current X interval or the exact rational value 1.
11.2.4 The selected seed shall be compatible with Newton convergence.

11.3 Only certified client-visible terms shall enter the ourorobos feedback stream.
11.3.1 Each emitted term shall be fed back immediately after certification.
11.3.2 The ourorobos shall accumulate the certified term and the range certified at that point.
11.3.3 No uncertified term shall be appended to the ourorobos.

11.4 The Y approximation stream shall be formed by feeding back emitted output through PQStreamFromRCF(ourorobos).
11.4.1 Before the first emitted term, the active Y approximation shall be the seed.
11.4.2 After feedback begins, Y terms shall come from PQStreamFromRCF(ourorobos).

11.5 The sqrt controller shall preserve Y-ingestion progress when X is refined.
11.5.1 The implementation shall maintain a split representation or exact equivalent separating n,d dependence from Y-derived factors.
11.5.2 When X is refined from n/d to n'/d', the controller shall update the X-dependent part of DLFT state without reinitializing the full DLFT.
11.5.3 Refining X shall not discard already ingested Y-term progress.

11.6 Sqrt output certification shall follow the general rectifier rule on the current DLFT image.
11.6.1 A term shall be emitted only when the rectifier proves a single integer floor over the full admissible image.
11.6.2 Certification attempts shall be observable in WB tests.

11.7 Sqrt progress shall follow the required cycle.
11.7.1 The controller shall first attempt certification from the current DLFT-plus-rectifier state.
11.7.2 If certification fails, the controller shall ingest the next Y term from the ourorobos or seed path.
11.7.3 If Y is exhausted and certification still fails, the controller shall ingest one more X term, refine the X estimate, and update DLFT state.
11.7.4 If the ingest budget is exceeded before certifying the next term, the controller shall return an explicit error.

11.8 Rational approximation of X for sqrt coefficient selection shall come from the current certified X interval.
11.8.1 SmallestRationalInInterval shall be used for nontrivial intervals.
11.8.2 Any selected n/d used for sqrt coefficient state shall satisfy n/d > 0.

11.9 The following sqrt internals shall be WB-visible.
11.9.1 sqrtController.ourorobos shall be directly observable in white-box tests.
11.9.2 sqrtController.hasOurorobosFeedback shall be directly observable in white-box tests.
11.9.3 The DLFT state or coefficients after each Newton step shall be directly observable in white-box tests.
11.9.4 The seed approximation before feedback begins shall be directly observable in white-box tests.
11.9.5 Whether certification was attempted with the current state shall be directly observable in white-box tests.

11.10 The initial BB signoff suite for sqrt shall include the required exact and streaming cases.
11.10.1 sqrt(0) shall produce exact 0.
11.10.2 sqrt(1) shall produce exact 1.
11.10.3 sqrt(4) shall produce exact 2.
11.10.4 sqrt(1/4) shall produce exact 1/2.
11.10.5 Negative exact input shall produce an error.
11.10.6 sqrt(2) for 50 terms shall match OEIS A040000, beginning [1, 2, 2, 2, 2, ...].
11.10.7 The live-feedback path for sqrt(2) shall be confirmed by WB tests rather than by shortcut behavior.

12. Conformance, Diagnostics, and Deferred-but-Preserved Extensions
12.1 The implementation shall remain conformant to the exact GCF/RCF architecture described by the governing spec set.
12.1.1 Where sqrt-specific docs conflict with older project notes, the Gosper-centered governing specs shall win.
12.1.2 Superseded design notes shall not override current approved requirements.

12.2 The design shall preserve a path for deferred decimal-digit emission.
12.2.1 Deferred decimal support shall not distort the exact-RCF-first architecture.
12.2.2 Decimal-oriented work may be postponed without weakening current exactness requirements.

12.3 The design should preserve room for Hurwitz-related diagnostics or optimizations.
12.3.1 Such future work shall remain subordinate to current correctness requirements.
12.3.2 Such future work shall not require architectural reversals of current exact-kernel design.

12.4 The implementation should expose useful debugging state for coefficients, ranges, and ingest decisions.
12.4.1 Diagnostics should help explain current kernel coefficients and admissible ranges.
12.4.2 Diagnostics should help explain why an ingest or emission decision was taken.

12.5 The design should preserve room for future symbolic sharing or memoized expression graphs.
12.5.1 Future sharing/memoization shall not require weakening the no-duplicate-consumption invariant.
12.5.2 Future sharing/memoization shall remain compatible with exact lazy evaluation.

12.6 Tests and documentation should demonstrate or preserve the best-approximation properties associated with HAKMEM 101A.
12.6.1 Demonstration of such properties shall not require speculative client-visible output.
12.6.2 Documentation of such properties shall remain consistent with certified-emission semantics.

12.7 The MVP conformance goal shall include the target expression sqrt(3/pi² + e) / (tanh(sqrt(5)) − sin(69°)).
12.7.1 The target expression shall be supported through the exact lazy operator stack rather than by a one-off shortcut.
12.7.2 Success on the target expression shall remain downstream of the foundational signoff requirements above.
